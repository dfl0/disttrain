Clock alignment

Aligned plots use root training start as zero. Remote clocks use startup/shutdown timestamp exchanges; linear correction is an estimate, not proof of constant drift. Error estimates describe endpoint network uncertainty, not unobserved clock changes between endpoints. Drift below that uncertainty is not distinguishable from measurement noise. Missing calibration is never silently treated as synchronized. Negative times can represent setup before root training.

Measurement overhead

  1. Check queue stalls first. They mean a producer waited for the recorder's queue to drain. Zero means no queue-full stalls were recorded, not zero instrumentation cost.
  2. Look at role-thread recording. This is time spent validating/enqueueing CSV records on the training/evaluation thread, including traces. It is the most direct recorded interference with role work.
  3. Inspect background costs separately. Resource sampling is wall time in the monitoring thread; writer CPU is CPU consumed by the CSV-writing thread. They can compete with training for CPU, but their times overlap with training and with each other.

Totals cover the recorded run, including setup/shutdown calls where applicable, not only training or the trace window. Each chart has a zero-based axis with its own labelled scale; bars are not stacked. Compare similar-duration workloads; longer runs naturally accumulate more cost. Missing measurements are —, not zero.

Resource sampler totals include its resource/temperature recording calls as well as querying resources. Queue stalls are already included in recording-call wall times, except shutdown-marker waits: do not add them again. There is no valid “total slowdown” obtained by adding these columns or dividing their sum by training elapsed. Timing/context-manager bookkeeping, calibration exchanges and other call-site work are not fully covered. To measure actual slowdown, repeat the same workload with instrumentation off/on and compare elapsed time and quality.

Recording-time fraction is not slowdown: it divides role-thread recording-call wall time by that node's recorder lifetime (creation to close request, including setup/waiting/shutdown but excluding final writer drain). It excludes console printing, background work and unmeasured instrumentation bookkeeping. Older runs without the measured recorder window show —; root training time is not substituted as a different denominator.
